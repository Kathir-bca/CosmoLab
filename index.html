Special relativity, worked live

Time dilation

A moving clock's own (proper) time runs slower than the time measured by a stationary observer, by a factor of γ.

Δt = γ·Δτ
    —
    time elapsed for the stationary observer

    Length contraction

    An object's length, measured in the direction of motion, appears shorter to an observer it moves past.

    L = L0 / γ
      —
      observed length

      Relativistic travel

      Compare how much time passes on Earth versus aboard a spacecraft crossing interstellar distances near light speed.

        —
        Earth-frame travel time vs. spacecraft proper time

        The two times differ because time and simultaneity depend on the observer's reference frame — not because either clock is "wrong."

        Lorentz factor at a glance

        γ stays close to 1 at everyday speeds and diverges only as v approaches c — which is why relativistic effects are invisible in daily life but dominant near light speed.

          Doppler shift

          Motion between source and observer shifts the wavelength of light — stretching it (redshift) when receding, compressing it (blueshift) when approaching.

          —
          apparent wavelength (reference: 550 nm green light)

          This uses the simplified non-relativistic Doppler approximation, valid well below light speed.
