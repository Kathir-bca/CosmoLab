🌌 Big Bang

The Big Bang is the leading cosmological model describing the evolution of the universe from an extremely hot, dense early state to the expanding, structured universe we observe today. It is not best understood as an ordinary explosion at one location in pre-existing space.

🎬 Video

Original video hosted by YouTube/National Geographic. CosmoLab embeds the original video rather than copying or re-uploading it.

📖 What is it?

The Big Bang model describes a universe that was much hotter and denser in the past. As the universe expanded, its temperature and density decreased. This changing environment allowed new physical processes to occur, from the formation of light nuclei to atoms, stars, galaxies, and larger cosmic structures.

The age of the universe is approximately 13.8 billion years. The model describes the universe's evolution extremely successfully, but it does not give us a complete physical description of an absolute beginning. In particular, treating a mathematical singularity from classical general relativity as a literal physical point would go beyond what current physics can establish.

⚙️ How does it work?

Very early universe

Many modern cosmological models include an extremely rapid early expansion called cosmic inflation. Its physical mechanism remains unknown.

Hot early universe

After the inflationary phase, the universe was an extremely hot environment containing radiation and particles. Expansion caused it to cool.

First minutes

Nuclear reactions produced light nuclei, primarily hydrogen and helium, with small amounts of other light nuclei.

About 380,000 years

Electrons combined with nuclei to form mostly neutral atoms. The universe became transparent and photons could travel freely. That ancient radiation is observed today as the CMB.

❓ Why does it matter?

The Big Bang model connects several independent observations into one coherent history. It explains why the universe was hotter and denser in the past, why cosmic background radiation exists, why the early universe produced the observed light elements, and how small early density variations could grow into the large-scale structure of galaxies and clusters.

🔬 Evidence

🌠 Cosmic expansion

Distant galaxies show systematic redshifts associated with cosmic expansion. Looking backward in an expanding universe leads to a hotter and denser past.

📡 Cosmic Microwave Background

The CMB is relic radiation from when the universe became transparent about 380,000 years after the Big Bang. Its near-uniformity and tiny fluctuations provide detailed information about the early universe.

⚛️ Light-element abundances

Big Bang nucleosynthesis predicts the production of the light nuclei, especially hydrogen and helium, in the hot early universe. Observed primordial abundances provide an important test.

🌌 Large-scale structure

Tiny density variations visible in the CMB provide seeds that gravity could amplify into galaxies, clusters, and the cosmic web.

🔗 Related concepts

❓ Common questions

Was the Big Bang an explosion?

No. The model describes the expansion and evolution of the universe itself, rather than an explosion from one location into pre-existing space.

Where did the Big Bang happen?

The standard cosmological picture does not identify a special center from which the universe expanded. Expansion occurs throughout space.

What happened before the Big Bang?

There is no confirmed answer. Some theoretical models explore earlier phases, but current observations do not establish one as fact.

Did everything begin as an infinitely small point?

Not as an established physical fact. A singularity can appear when classical general relativity is extrapolated to extreme conditions, but our current theories are incomplete there.

Is the universe still expanding?

Yes. Observations show cosmic expansion, and the expansion is currently accelerating.

What is the universe expanding into?

The standard model does not require an external space for the universe to expand into. The distances between sufficiently separated objects increase as the geometry of space evolves.

How do we know the Big Bang happened?

No single observation proves the whole model. The case comes from multiple independent observations, especially cosmic expansion, the CMB, primordial light-element abundances, and large-scale structure.
